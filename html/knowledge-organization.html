Knowledge Organization

Theoretical Model

The theoretical model renders the ideas extracted from the Wikipedia page into a triple format. The statements are still expressed in natural language.

The items are coded on basis of their types: individuals, places, items, concepts, events.

TEI/XML Document Modeling

A curated selection of the English Wikipedia article on Tiresias was encoded conforming to the TEI P5 Guidelines. Passages were sampled according to their relevance to the research dimensions (transformation, divine dispute, prophetic foresight, and literary reception). Omitted passages are explicitly marked using <gap reason="sampling">.

The <teiHeader> records the primary source, annotates entities (persons, places, literary and visual works, mythological events), and models relationships in a formal <listRelation> structure, where @active, @name, and @passive map directly to RDF subjects, predicates, and objects.

Conceptual Model

On basis of the theoretical model, the conceptual model converts the the natural-language statements into RDF classes, properties and URIs. Authoritative ontologies were utilized when possible, to allow for re-usability. The default prefix is set to the local ontology, available in the repository; in which the local predicates are defined.

For readabilty purposes, here's a list of the local predicates used:

Object Predicate Descrpition
hasGift Relates a mythological figure to a supernatural ability or faculty bestowed upon it, such as foresight or prophecy.
isPreconditionedBy Relates an event to an earlier event without which it could not have taken place. Since a precondition necessarily precedes what it makes possible, the property is a specialisation of CIDOC CRM 'occurs after'.
resultsIn Relates an event to a concept (a condition, faculty or state) that comes into being as its outcome.
prophesiedAbout Relates a seer to a mythological figure whose fate was the subject of one of the seer's prophecies.
illustrates Relates a visual artwork to a textual work it was produced to accompany or illustrate, e.g. an engraving published in an edition of that work.
